Hi, I'm Whitney

I know someone who could help you make sense of this.

That's the sentence I want people to say when they think of me. I'm Whitney Lovett, a Licensed Professional Counselor and Certified Clinical Trauma Professional in Denver, and I've spent sixteen years in the parts of behavioral health most people only see from the waiting room.

Where I've been

Sixteen years with the people the system finds hardest to serve.

Most of my career has been with adults living with serious mental illness, substance use disorders, chronic homelessness, and involvement with the criminal justice system. I started in direct clinical care and community mental health, moved into program development and clinical leadership, and ended up in executive roles responsible for whole teams and the services they delivered.

My last leadership role was building a behavioral health program inside a nonprofit that serves people returning to the community after incarceration. That meant standing up clinical services from scratch, getting Medicaid billing working, supervising clinicians, writing the processes, and training staff across the organization.

What I learned there, and in every setting before it, is that the diagnosis is rarely the hardest part. The hardest part is the maze: the hospital that discharges on a Friday, the program with a six-week waitlist, the probation officer and the case manager who have never spoken to each other. I got good at the maze. That turned out to be the most useful thing I know.

Why I built this

A smaller caseload and a wider reach.

After years in high-acuity settings and leadership, I wanted a more sustainable professional life, and I wanted to use everything I'd learned rather than just one slice of it. So this practice does three things on purpose.

I keep a small, steady caseload of individual and couples therapy, because I like the room and I'm good in it. I offer consultation to families who are dealing with a loved one's mental illness, addiction, or time in the justice system, because almost nobody does and the need is enormous. And I train and consult with organizations on trauma-informed practice, program development, and staff resilience, because the teams doing this work deserve support too.

I'd rather be known in a handful of communities as the person who can explain the complicated thing than be everywhere at once. That's the plan.

How I work

Collaborative, relational, trauma-informed, systems-aware.

Those four words shape everything. Collaborative means we figure this out together; I'm not handing you a fix. Relational means the connection between us is part of the work. Trauma-informed means we move at a pace your nervous system can hold, and you decide what gets talked about and when. Systems-aware means I look at the family, the agency, the court, and the culture around a problem, because that's usually where the problem lives.

Some people need help processing what they feel. Some people are overwhelmed, scared, and confused, and what they actually need first is someone to say: here's what this means, here are the options, let's figure out your next step. I do both, and I'll tell you honestly which one I think you need.

A good number of the people who find me have had reasons not to trust the places that were supposed to help, and have been called difficult for learning that. That's not a problem to manage here. It's usually where the real work starts.

Who I work with

Individuals, couples, families, and the organizations around them.

Adults carrying trauma, anxiety, depression, burnout, grief, identity shifts, and the big transitions. Couples working on communication, reconnection, and the pattern they keep landing in. Families supporting someone through a psychiatric hospitalization, a treatment program, a long illness, or a sentence. Schools, nonprofits, treatment programs, and helping professionals who want their teams to last.

I'm also interested in building relationships with treatment centers, recovery programs, and community organizations that meet families in these moments. If that's you, here's how I work with professionals.
