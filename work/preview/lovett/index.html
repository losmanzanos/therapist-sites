Hi, I'm Whitney

The short version.

Licensed Professional Counselor (MA, LPC) and Certified Clinical Trauma Professional (CCTP) in Denver. I spent most of sixteen years in behavioral health working with people living with serious mental illness, addiction, homelessness, and the criminal justice system: in clinics, in community mental health, and most recently building and leading a behavioral health program for people coming home after incarceration.

That work taught me two things. Mental health conditions are only half the problem. The other half is the maze of systems people have to get through to find treatment, housing, and support, and almost nobody explains the maze.

So that's what I do now. Therapy for adults and couples, paced for the nervous system you actually have. Consultation for families who need someone to explain what's happening and help them plan. Training for the teams who carry this work every day. No clipboards, no worksheets that look like the SAT, and nobody rushing you.

For families

When it's someone you love, and nobody is explaining anything.

Your daughter was admitted on a 72-hour hold and you don't know what happens on day four. Your brother just got out and he's sleeping on your couch. Your husband's program keeps saying "IOP" like you should know what that is.

Families in these situations get a lot of pamphlets and very few straight answers. I've worked inside the hospitals, the treatment programs, and the reentry system, and I can tell you what the words mean, what to expect, where the resources are, and how to set a boundary you can actually keep.

This isn't an emergency line. It's a scheduled conversation with someone who has seen the inside of the thing you're dealing with.

How this works.

Reach out · Get to it · Keep going

i.

The first call

  • Fifteen minutes, free, no pitch deck and no pressure
  • You tell me what's loud, what's stuck, and what you've already tried
  • I tell you honestly whether I'm the right fit, or who is
ii.

The actual work

  • Weekly or biweekly for therapy; one session or a short series for family consultation
  • Pacing that honors your nervous system and your life
  • Goals named in plain language and revisited like they matter
iii.

The keep-going

  • Every 8–12 weeks we check the map against the territory
  • Recalibrate when life rearranges itself, because it always does
  • Wrap when you're ready, with tools you know how to use
A few things you can count on

What I won't do.

I won't ask you to describe the worst thing that ever happened to you. Not in the first session, and not until you decide you want to.

I won't hand you a diagnosis on the way out the door, or tell you what to do about your marriage or your job after fifty minutes.

I won't be taking notes the whole time with my eyes on a screen.

And if it turns out I'm not the right fit, I'll say so and give you other names. You don't owe me an explanation for not coming back.

A note from me

You shouldn't have to hide the complicated parts to deserve help.

Mental illness, addiction, a family member in jail, a marriage coming apart. These things are far more common than people say out loud, and the shame around them keeps a lot of people from asking for help until things are much worse. I built this practice to be the place where you can say the whole thing, plainly, and get a plain answer back.
