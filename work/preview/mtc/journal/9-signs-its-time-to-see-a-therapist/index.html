9 Signs It’s Time to See a Therapist

August 5, 2026 · 6 min read · Montclair Therapy Collective

Are you wondering, “Do I need therapy?”

It’s understandable to be uncertain about when to seek mental health support. Therapy is portrayed in media as something for people who are totally out of control, or for people who are just interested in talking about themselves, and you probably don’t identify with either of these stereotypes.

Thankfully, the conversation around when to see a therapist is evolving away from old stereotypes and stigmas. Therapy is better understood now as a space that can be useful for most people. In therapy, you can work through trauma, grief, mental health symptoms, major life events, relationship challenges, parenting concerns, stress, and more.

All of us can relate to at least some, if not all, of the challenges that lead people to seek therapy, and it’s not only reasonable, it’s sensible to reach out for support when you’re having a tough time.

Still unsure if you should go to therapy? Let’s review 9 signs you should see a therapist.

1You’ve received a mental health diagnosis and want more support

A major sign you need counseling is having received a mental health diagnosis and realizing you need more support as you navigate your new understanding of what you’ve been going through. Whether you’ve completed a residential, PHP, or IOP mental health program and are looking for consistent outpatient therapist support, or you’ve received your diagnosis from a doctor or psychiatrist and are starting your treatment process from the beginning, a therapist is an important member of your support system.

A therapist’s place in your treatment plan is as a consistent, supportive presence in your life, helping you keep track of your symptoms and progress week to week. They will use specialized knowledge and evidence-based treatment approaches to help you cope with symptoms in healthy, constructive ways.

They can also help you learn about your mental health condition in a way that destigmatizes it for you, supporting you as you process how you feel about having a name for what you’ve been experiencing.

2You’re stressed out all the time

You don’t need a diagnosis to reach out for therapy. If you’re noticing that your stress levels are constantly high, a therapist in Montclair, NJ can help you.

A therapist can help you pinpoint the reasons for your stress and help you put into motion practical ways to reduce your stress. Whether it’s stronger boundaries, a different mindset, focusing on self-care, or making major changes to your life, stress management is a great reason to seek mental health support.

3You’ve gone through something difficult

Whether you’ve just had an experience that’s sticking with you, or you went through something weeks, months, or years ago and you’re realizing it’s still impacting your life, a therapist near Montclair, NJ can help.

When you’ve gone through something traumatic, you need someone to talk to, to understand what you’ve experienced and how to move forward. Your therapist can help you process your experiences, reducing the hold the events of your past have on your life today.

4You’ve noticed unhelpful patterns in your life, and you want to change them

A string of bad relationships, a series of job losses, friendships that start out great and fizzle out fast, having the same arguments over and over with your partner, picking up hobbies and dropping them just as fast, moving from place to place without ever feeling settled.

When you notice some consistent patterns in your life that you know are making your life harder, and you’re struggling to figure out how to change the pattern, it’s a sign you should see a therapist. Your therapist can help you understand the “why” behind these patterns, and can give you concrete, actionable ways to notice, stop, and change these life patterns, so you feel more empowered to build a stable life without toxic patterns.

5You’re worried about certain behaviors and want to change them

You may notice certain behaviors you resort to that you don’t like. They may disrupt your life, injure you, risk your health and wellbeing, or harm others. They may also be your only outlet for soothing overwhelming feelings like anxiety, fear, sadness, or emptiness, and the thought of changing them may be frightening.

A therapist can help you change these behaviors while also changing how you cope with the thoughts and feelings that are at the root of the behaviors. You won’t be left without help while trying to change your life; your therapist will be right there with you, helping you cope while also holding you accountable.

Therapy can help with:

  • Impulsive spending or gambling
  • Hoarding
  • Reckless behavior like dangerous driving, substance use, or sexual behaviors
  • Avoiding people, places, experiences, and items to reduce anxiety
  • Repetitive behaviors that are a response to overwhelming anxiety and intrusive thoughts
  • Self-harm
  • Repetitive picking, biting, or hair pulling

6You feel terrible about yourself

You may feel less-than, inferior, or like an impostor. Like you’re worthless, unattractive, or a joke. You may insult yourself regularly, maybe even making jokes at your own expense to cover up your hurt. When you feel this way, it’s time to see a therapist.

Feeling terrible about yourself isn’t something you have to just live with; you weren’t born feeling bad about yourself, you learned it along the way. A therapist in Montclair, NJ can help you figure out why you feel this way, and can empower you to adjust your mindset and take steps to rebuild your self-esteem and self-confidence.

7You feel angry, sad, empty, scared, etc. all the time

Feeling intense emotions like anger, sorrow, or fear in response to infuriating, sad, or frightening events makes sense. When you start feeling these emotions a lot of the time, in ways that aren’t proportionate to what’s going on around you, it’s time to go to therapy.

Your therapist can help you figure out what you’re feeling and how to cope when emotions flare up. They can help you understand the reasons for your chronic anger, fear, or emptiness, and can connect you with a broader care team in Montclair if needed.

8You feel stuck

When you feel like you need to change something in your life, but you aren’t sure what, or you know what needs to change, but not how to do it, it’s time to see a therapist in Montclair, NJ.

A therapist is a neutral, supportive person in your life who can hear you out without judgment or insisting you go one way or the other in life. In therapy, you won’t be told what to do; you’ll be given the tools to figure out what you want to do, and how to do it. Your therapist can help you see that you do have the ability to change your life, supporting you to uncover the values and goals that will lead you toward the right answer, for you.

9You’re going through a major life change

Divorce, marriage, retirement, a new baby, a blended family, a breakup, a new job, moving house, and other major life events can be hard to handle on your own, even if it’s a life change you want.

Coping with change is easier with support, and therapy can be a place to talk through how you feel, to make sense of conflicting feelings and a changing sense of self, and a place where your therapist can help you decide what you want to do next.

Is it time to seek mental health support? Montclair Therapy Collective can connect you to the right therapist for you

When you’re struggling, it’s not always easy to know if what you’re going through is a sign you need counseling. It’s important to know that if you feel like you need to reach out and seek mental health support in Montclair, you’re probably right.

A good therapist will invite you to ask questions and share what you’re experiencing, and they will help you make sense of what you’re going through, giving you a good idea of when therapy is right for you.
