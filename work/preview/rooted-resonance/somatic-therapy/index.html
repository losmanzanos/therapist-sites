What somatic therapy actually is

Somatic therapy treats the body as a source of information rather than a problem to manage. Talk therapy works on the story you tell about your life. Somatic work goes underneath it, to the physical patterns that formed before you had words, and that keep running whether or not you understand them.

In practice that means slowing down. Noticing where a sentence lands in your chest. Staying with a sensation long enough for it to change on its own instead of being explained away. It is less dramatic than people expect and more effective than most of them are prepared for.

Who this tends to help

  • People who have been in therapy before and left with excellent insight and unchanged behavior.
  • People carrying developmental or relational trauma that predates language.
  • Chronic anxiety, freeze, shutdown, or a nervous system stuck on high alert.
  • Anyone who intellectualizes as a first defense, and knows they are doing it.
  • People pleasers who learned to earn love by adapting and are worn out from it. More on therapy for people pleasing.
  • LGBTQIA+, neurodivergent, kinky, and ethically non monogamous clients who want a therapist who does not need the basics explained. More on affirming therapy.

What a session looks like

We talk, but not the whole time. I will ask what you notice in your body, and if the answer is nothing, that is useful information rather than a wrong answer. We work at the pace your nervous system can tolerate, which is a real limit and not a matter of willingness. Some sessions are conversational. Some are quiet.

Questions people ask before starting

Can somatic therapy be done over video?

Yes. Therapy is offered by telehealth for residents of Ohio, Arizona and Colorado. Counseling licenses are state by state, so those three are the only states where I can offer it.

Most of the work is noticing: breath, posture, the reflex to brace. None of that needs us in the same room.

Do I have to talk about my trauma?

No. You set the pace, and nothing gets opened before there is enough support for it. If you notice nothing in your body at first, we start there.

What does it cost, and can I use insurance?

$150 a session, self pay. I am in network with Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, Medical Mutual of Ohio, United Healthcare and several others, some of them through Headway or Rula. The full list is on the FAQ.

Coverage varies by plan and by state, so it is worth confirming your own benefits.

What happens after I reach out?

I reply, usually within two business days. If it looks like a fit, we set up a short conversation at no cost. If it does not, I will say so and try to point you somewhere useful. Send a note here.
