Stage I of Five

Awaken

Something doesn't feel right, and that's where your journey begins.

IAwakenNoticeIIUnderstandRecognizeIIIReconnectTrustIVBecomeDiscoverVRelateChoose

Most people don't begin healing because they wake up one morning feeling inspired. They begin because something no longer fits.

You keep trying to change things, but it never feels like enough. You are exhausted from holding it all together. You spend your time exhausting yourself for others, and never knowing how to slow down for yourself. Throughout your days and nights you have held a quiet sense that there has to be another way to live.

You don't need to know exactly what's wrong to begin. You only need the willingness to become curious.

Awakening isn't about getting to the answer. It's about allowing yourself to ask new questions.

What is happening

Before we ever know there is a problem, when things feel like normal, change seems unnecessary.

Many of the patterns that helped you survive now feel limiting. Over time your nervous system learned how to adapt to your environment in order to create safety, belonging and acceptance.

These patterns and adaptations were intelligent. They helped you navigate the world with the resources you had.

The goal is not to judge the patterns or how you got here. The goal is to learn about them, get curious, and seek understanding.

How we work together

Together, we'll slow down enough to notice what your mind and body have been trying to communicate. Through compassionate exploration, we'll begin identifying the patterns that no longer serve you while honoring the ways they've protected you.

What usually brings people here

Rarely a crisis. More often an accumulation. A relationship that keeps circling the same argument, a job that has quietly stopped fitting, a body that has started making its objections physical. Something small breaks the surface and you realize you have been managing rather than living.

Some people arrive able to name it precisely. Most cannot, and arrive slightly embarrassed that they cannot. That is not a problem to solve before we start. Not knowing what is wrong is the ordinary condition of this stage, and it is workable.

What tends to get in the way

  • Waiting until things are bad enough to justify it. They rarely become conveniently bad. They become slowly worse.
  • Deciding you should be able to sort it out alone, which is often the same self reliance that got you here.
  • Comparing your situation to someone with a harder one and concluding you have no standing to complain.
  • Assuming that because you are functioning, nothing needs attention. Functioning and being well are not the same measurement.

What the first conversation is like

Twenty minutes, free, no obligation. You say some of it out loud, probably imprecisely, and I listen for what is underneath. I will tell you honestly whether I think I am the right person. If I am not, I will say so and point you somewhere better.

Nothing gets decided in that call. You are not committing to a course of work, and you do not need to arrive with your history organized.
