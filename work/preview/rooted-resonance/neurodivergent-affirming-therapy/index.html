Masking is an adaptation, and this practice is built around adaptations.

Masking, from this side of the room

Masking is what this whole practice is about, under a different name. Watching how other people do it. Adjusting. Getting it mostly right, and paying for it later. It is the same move as adapting to keep a relationship or avoid a conflict, and it usually started for the same reasons.

Since 2020 my private practice has served LGBTQIA+, neurodivergent and kinky communities. You will not need to translate how your mind works before we can get to what you came in with.

The translating can stop at the door.

What people bring

  • Burnout from years of trying to fit in, sometimes without a name for it.
  • Watching everyone else to work out how you are supposed to act.
  • Not knowing which parts of you are you and which are the performance.
  • Being too much in one room and not enough in the next.
  • Relationships that look fine from outside and take everything you have to keep up.

Some of that work happens in somatic therapy and some in relational therapy. You can move between the two.

Why start with the body

If you have spent years analyzing social situations to get through them, you are probably very good at explaining yourself. Somatic work goes underneath the explanation, to the reflexes that keep running whether or not you understand them.

I will ask what you notice in your body. If the answer is nothing, that is useful information rather than a wrong answer. We go at the pace your nervous system can tolerate, and some sessions are mostly quiet.

Where this is available

Therapy is by telehealth for residents of Ohio, Arizona and Colorado. Sessions are $150 self pay, with several plans accepted, including Aetna, Cigna and United Healthcare, some through Headway or Rula. The full insurance list is on the FAQ, and self pay clients can ask for a Good Faith Estimate.

If you are also queer, kinky or non monogamous, affirming therapy covers that side of it.

Practical answers

Do I need a diagnosis to work with you?

No. You do not need a diagnosis or a referral to start therapy. You can reach out directly through the contact form.

Can we do this over video?

Yes. Therapy is by telehealth for residents of Ohio, Arizona and Colorado. A room where you will not be interrupted for the hour helps more than any particular setup.

Is this only for people who are also queer or kinky?

No. Neurodivergent clients are welcome whatever the rest of their life looks like. If you are queer, kinky or non monogamous as well, you will not have to explain that either.

How do I start?

Send a short note. Keep it general, a sentence or two is plenty. I reply, usually within two business days, and if it looks like a fit we set up a short conversation at no cost.
