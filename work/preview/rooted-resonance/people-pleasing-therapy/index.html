Therapy for
people pleasing

For people who got very good at being who everyone needed, and lost track of who they are underneath.

It made sense at the time

Most people pleasing starts as good judgment. Somewhere early on you worked out that being easy to be around kept things calm, or kept love coming, or kept you out of the line of fire. So you got good at it. Very good.

I know this one from the inside. I have spent my life living out the patterns of adapting, people pleasing and self abandonment, not because I wanted to, but because that is what I thought I needed to do to survive. The question that came out of it, who am I when I am no longer performing for belonging, is where this practice started.

The skill still works. It just costs more than it used to.

How it shows up by the time people reach out

It rarely looks like being too nice. More often it looks like this:

  • You get very agreeable exactly when you are most upset.
  • You read the room before you decide what you feel.
  • Saying no feels like betrayal, even when it is clearly the right call.
  • Rest only feels allowed once everyone else is taken care of.
  • Conflict feels like a threat to the whole relationship.
  • You are tired in a way a weekend does not fix.

If you can explain the pattern perfectly and still do it, you are in good company. Plenty of people arrive having done therapy before, with excellent insight and the same behavior. That gap is not a lack of effort. Insight lives in one part of the brain, and the pattern runs in another.

Where the work happens

The automatic yes is fast, and it happens in the body first. A tight throat, a held breath, a smile that shows up before you have decided anything. In somatic therapy we slow that moment down until you can feel it starting, which is the first place you get a choice.

Then the new answer has to survive real people. Relational therapy is where it gets tried with the people who matter, in relationships of any shape and with people working through them on their own. Workshops are a room to practice it with others watching, which is exactly where the old pull is strongest.

Nothing gets pushed. We work at the pace your nervous system can tolerate, and that limit is real.

Who can book this

Therapy is by telehealth for residents of Ohio, Arizona and Colorado, the three states where I am licensed. Sessions are $150 self pay. I am in network with Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, Medical Mutual of Ohio, United Healthcare and others, some through Headway or Rula, and the full list is on the FAQ. Paying out of pocket? You can ask for a Good Faith Estimate.

Outside those states, breathwork and workshops are open to you. They are wellness services, not therapy.

If you are on the fence

Is people pleasing a trauma response?

It can be. For a lot of people it started as a way to stay safe or stay loved. I treat it as an adaptation that once protected you, and the work is about what it is still protecting.

What if I start people pleasing my therapist?

You probably will, at least early on. When it shows up in the room we get to look at it while it is happening, which beats talking about it a week later.

Do I need a diagnosis or a referral?

No. You can reach out directly. A sentence or two through the contact form is enough.

I am not in Ohio, Arizona or Colorado. Is there anything for me?

Not therapy, because counseling licenses are state by state. Breathwork can be done remotely from anywhere, and workshops are open to anyone. Neither is psychotherapy.
