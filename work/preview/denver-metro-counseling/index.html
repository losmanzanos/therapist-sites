Notes for Julie

Lead with people, not a list.

Today the first screen is a list of eight conditions and six methods. Every practice in Denver can say that. The team photo and one honest paragraph say who you are before anyone reads a diagnosis.

Your own words.

"Past the fire of the week" comes from your About page. "We all have the answers we need" is from your bio. The voice is already on the site; it's just buried four clicks deep.

Three doors instead of three lists.

Individuals, families and therapists each get one line in the language they'd use, and one link. The full specialty list moves to the pages it belongs on, where it helps search instead of crowding the homepage.

Built for the SEO and Ads teams.

One clear main heading with Denver in it. Self-pay and ages stated up front, so ad clicks that will never book filter themselves out. Each ad should point to its own landing page, hidden from the menu, with form submissions and phone taps tracked, so you can see exactly what the ad spend brings in.

On the video.

The current hero is a stock clip of a stranger on a trail, about 4.6 MB, playing behind white text. It's the heaviest thing on the page, it slows phones down, and it doesn't show anyone a client will actually meet. A still photo of the real team loads instantly and does the one job the top of the page has: helping someone feel they've found the right people.
