Sound familiar?

The anxiety that looks like ambition.

High-functioning anxiety rarely gets noticed, because it gets results. Until it doesn't.

You rewrite the email four times. You rehearse the conversation in the shower. You lie awake running through what you said at dinner.

From the outside it looks like conscientiousness. Inside, it feels like there is no margin for error, ever. Rest feels like falling behind. Compliments slide off and criticism sticks for weeks.

For some people it shows up as social anxiety: skipping the party, staying quiet in class or meetings, dreading being seen. For others it is burnout, where the drive that used to carry you is just gone and you can't remember the last time you felt rested.

Perfectionism is often the engine under all of it. It promises that if you get everything right, you will finally feel okay. You probably already know it doesn't keep that promise.

What changes in therapy

Keep the drive. Lose the dread.

We start by figuring out what your anxiety is protecting you from and what it is costing you. Then we work on it two ways.

CBT helps you catch the thinking traps, like catastrophizing, mind-reading, and all-or-nothing standards, and practice responses that are more accurate. ACT helps you stop waiting for anxiety to disappear before you live your life, and start acting on what matters to you while it is still there.

Exposure is part of it too. If you have been avoiding things (speaking up, sending work before it's perfect, social plans), we practice them in small steps. Avoidance keeps anxiety alive.

Along the way we work on softening the inner critic and building real self-compassion. Not as a slogan. As a skill you practice until it's automatic.

Questions

Fair questions.

Something else on your mind? Ask it on the free call, or text (843) 212-6965.

How do I know if it is anxiety or just stress?

Stress usually lifts when the deadline passes. Anxiety tends to find the next thing. If worry is taking up hours of your day, costing you sleep, or making you avoid things you used to do, it is worth talking about.

Do you work with teens?

Yes. Teens today are dealing with more comparison and pressure than ever, and anxiety and perfectionism often start in high school. See teens, children and parents.

Will you tell me to just lower my standards?

No. Your drive is part of who you are. The goal is to keep what works about it and drop the part where nothing is ever enough.

Do you prescribe medication?

No. We are therapists, not prescribers. If medication might help, we can talk about it and coordinate with your doctor or psychiatrist.
