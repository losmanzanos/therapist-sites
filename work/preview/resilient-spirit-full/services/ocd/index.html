The thought isn't the problem. What you do to make it stop is.

Intrusive thoughts feel urgent, disgusting or terrifying, so you check, ask, wash, review or avoid until they quiet down. It works for an hour. Then they come back louder. ERP breaks that loop, and it is the treatment with the strongest evidence for OCD.

How OCD works

Why reassurance never lasts.

ObsessionAn unwanted thought, image or urge
AnxietyIt feels urgent and dangerous
CompulsionCheck, ask, wash, review, avoid
ReliefFor an hour, until it comes back louder

Every trip around the loop teaches your brain the thought was dangerous.

Almost everyone with OCD has tried to reason with it. That is usually how it gets stronger.

OCD has two parts. An obsession, which is a thought, image or urge you don't want. And a compulsion, which is anything you do to get rid of the anxiety it brings.

Some compulsions are visible: washing, checking the stove, rereading a text before sending it. Many are not. Mentally reviewing a conversation. Googling symptoms. Asking your partner if they're sure. Replacing a "bad" thought with a "good" one. Avoiding knives, or kids, or a certain number.

Each compulsion brings relief, and the relief teaches your brain that the thought was dangerous and the ritual is what kept you safe. So the next thought hits harder and the ritual has to be bigger.

The content of the thoughts is often the part people are most ashamed of: harm, sexuality, religion, relationships. Those themes are common in OCD. Having the thought does not mean you want it, and nothing you say in session will surprise us.

Forms OCD takes

Same loop, different content.

You don't need to fit neatly into one of these. Most people don't.

Contamination

Germs, illness, chemicals, or a feeling of being "dirty" that washing never quite fixes.

Harm

Unwanted images of hurting someone, or fear that you might lose control or already have.

Checking and doubt

Locks, stoves, emails, homework, driving routes. Checking once is never enough.

"Just right"

Things have to be even, ordered, or repeated until they feel right, not just look right.

Relationships

Constant questioning of whether you love your partner enough, or whether they are the right one.

Scrupulosity and morality

Fear of sinning, lying, or being a bad person, with confessing and reassurance-seeking to match.

Exposure and Response Prevention

What ERP actually looks like.

ERP has a reputation for being harsh. Done well, it is a ladder you build together and climb one rung at a time. You are never forced into anything.

  1. Rung one

    Name the loop

    We map your obsessions and every compulsion attached to them, including the mental ones you may not have noticed.

  2. Rung two

    Build the ladder

    Together we rank situations from mildly uncomfortable to very hard. You start near the bottom.

  3. Rung three

    Face it, skip the ritual

    You approach a step on purpose and let the anxiety rise and fall on its own, without checking, asking or neutralizing.

  4. Rung four

    Climb, and keep it

    As each rung gets easier you move up. We finish with a plan for the spikes that will still come, so a bad week doesn't turn into a relapse.

Questions

Questions people ask about ERP.

Something else on your mind? Ask it on the free call, or text (843) 212-6965.

How long does ERP take?

It depends on how long OCD has been running things and how many areas it touches. ERP is structured and goal-driven rather than open-ended, and we track progress as we go so you can see whether it is working.

Can ERP be done over video?

Yes. Telehealth works well for ERP, and it has one real advantage: many exposures happen right where OCD shows up, in your kitchen, your car or your bedroom, instead of in an office.

My child has OCD. Do you treat kids?

Yes. We use ERP with children and teens, and we bring parents in, because family accommodation (answering the same question again, helping with rituals) usually keeps OCD going. SPACE is built for exactly that.

Is this the same as "being a little OCD"?

No. Plenty of people like things tidy. OCD is when unwanted thoughts and the rituals that follow take up real time, cause real distress, and get in the way of your life.
