Getting started

Do I have to live in South Carolina or North Carolina?

You need to be physically in SC or NC at the time of each session, because that is where Samantha is licensed. Visiting from another state for the session does not count, but living elsewhere and being here for school or work does.

What should I put in the form?

Just enough to start: your name, how to reach you, and a sentence about what is going on. Please keep detailed health information out of the form and save it for the call.

How fast will I hear back?

Within one business day. Texting (843) 212-6965 is usually the fastest way to reach us.

What if I am in crisis?

This form is not monitored around the clock. If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline), call 911, or go to your nearest emergency room.

Do you check my benefits?

Yes. Before your first session we verify your coverage and tell you what to expect to pay, so there are no surprises.

What if my plan is not listed?

You can pay the self-pay rate and request a superbill to submit for out-of-network reimbursement. Call your insurer and ask about "out-of-network outpatient mental health benefits" to find out what they cover.

Can I use my HSA or FSA?

Therapy is generally an eligible expense for HSA and FSA accounts. Check with your plan administrator to confirm.

Is the consult call really free?

Yes. The 15 minute consult is free, with no commitment.

Athletes and performers

Is this sport psychology?

Not exactly. Sport psychology usually focuses on mental skills for performance. This is therapy: we work on the anxiety, perfectionism, eating concerns or identity questions underneath the performance. Most athletes find their performance improves as a side effect, but the focus is on you, not your stats.

My child is a high school athlete. Can I be involved?

Yes. For clients under 18, a parent or guardian is part of the intake, and we talk together about what is shared and what stays between your teen and their therapist. If your athlete won't come to therapy, SPACE lets you work on it as a parent.

I play in college out of state. Can we still meet?

Sessions happen by video, and you need to be physically in South Carolina or North Carolina at the time of each session. If you are in school elsewhere, we can meet during breaks at home, or help you find someone licensed where you are.

Do you work with performers who aren't athletes?

Yes. Musicians, dancers, students facing high-stakes testing, and professionals who freeze before a big presentation often deal with the same pressure.

OCD and ERP

How long does ERP take?

It depends on how long OCD has been running things and how many areas it touches. ERP is structured and goal-driven rather than open-ended, and we track progress as we go so you can see whether it is working.

Can ERP be done over video?

Yes. Telehealth works well for ERP, and it has one real advantage: many exposures happen right where OCD shows up, in your kitchen, your car or your bedroom, instead of in an office.

My child has OCD. Do you treat kids?

Yes. We use ERP with children and teens, and we bring parents in, because family accommodation (answering the same question again, helping with rituals) usually keeps OCD going. SPACE is built for exactly that.

Is this the same as "being a little OCD"?

No. Plenty of people like things tidy. OCD is when unwanted thoughts and the rituals that follow take up real time, cause real distress, and get in the way of your life.

Eating disorders

Is online therapy enough for an eating disorder?

For many people, yes, especially when a physician is monitoring physical health and a dietitian is involved. If your medical picture needs closer care, we will tell you honestly and help you find it.

Do you work with teens and their parents?

Yes. With teens, parents are usually part of treatment. You see what is happening at meals in a way nobody else does.

I am an athlete and my coach thinks I am just "dialed in." Is this for me?

If you are scared of foods, training to make up for eating, or losing your period or your energy, it is worth a conversation. See our page on therapy for athletes.

What if I am not ready to change yet?

That is common and it is fine to start there. Part of early treatment is talking honestly about what you would be giving up.

Anxiety and perfectionism

How do I know if it is anxiety or just stress?

Stress usually lifts when the deadline passes. Anxiety tends to find the next thing. If worry is taking up hours of your day, costing you sleep, or making you avoid things you used to do, it is worth talking about.

Do you work with teens?

Yes. Teens today are dealing with more comparison and pressure than ever, and anxiety and perfectionism often start in high school. See teens, children and parents.

Will you tell me to just lower my standards?

No. Your drive is part of who you are. The goal is to keep what works about it and drop the part where nothing is ever enough.

Do you prescribe medication?

No. We are therapists, not prescribers. If medication might help, we can talk about it and coordinate with your doctor or psychiatrist.

Kids, teens and parents

How young are the children you see?

Ask us on the consult call. The right fit depends on your child's age and what is going on, and for younger kids, parent-focused work like SPACE is often where we start.

Do I need to be in the session?

For younger children, often yes, at least part of the time. For teens, sessions are usually one-on-one with parent check-ins. With SPACE, it is only you.

Will you tell me what my teen says?

Not word for word. Teens open up when they trust their sessions are private. You will hear about overall progress and anything involving their safety, and we agree on the details together at intake.

Do you take insurance for children?

Yes, the same plans we accept for adults. See fees and insurance.
