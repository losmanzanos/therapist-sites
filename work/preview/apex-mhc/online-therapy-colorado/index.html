Online therapy

Anywhere in Colorado, with a door that closes.

Every session at Apex happens online, for adults and couples anywhere in the state. What that takes, what it costs, and when it is the wrong fit.

There is no office. Someone at a kitchen table in Durango and someone on a lunch break in Lower Downtown get the same sixty minutes, the same clinician and the same fee.

The drive is gone, and so are the parking and the waiting room. That sounds like a convenience. In practice it is often the difference between going every week and quietly stopping in the second month.

Inside the state line

Denver and the suburbs around it, Boulder, Fort Collins and Greeley, Colorado Springs and Pueblo, the towns strung along I-70, the Western Slope, the San Luis Valley. If you are in Colorado when the session starts, you can be seen.

That part is a rule. I am licensed in Colorado, and the license goes by where you are sitting at the time, not where you live. Out of state on a session day? Tell me ahead of time and we will work out what is possible.

Your end of it

A private place to sit and a connection that holds.

Private matters more than people expect. If someone could be listening through the wall, you will edit yourself without meaning to, and the hour turns careful. A parked car works for a lot of people for exactly that reason. So does a bedroom with the door shut.

How it runs

  • A free fifteen-minute phone call first, to work out whether this is a fit
  • Sixty-minute sessions, weekly to start and less often once things are moving
  • Some evening times, which go quickly
  • $185 a session, with a sliding scale that is real
  • In network with Aetna, Anthem, Carelon, Cigna and Select Health, through Headway

Couples sessions run online as well. The Work walks through the first call, the first session and what a normal month looks like.

Is it as good as being in the room?

For most adults and most concerns, the research says yes. That includes trauma work and EMDR, which tends to surprise people. Some find the hard thing easier to say from their own chair than from a stranger’s couch.

The wrong fit

Telehealth is a poor container for someone who needs same-day, in-person care, and I do not provide crisis services. It also depends on that private place. If either is a problem right now, say so on the first call and I will point you somewhere better suited. I see adults eighteen and over, so this is not the place for children or teenagers.

Anything you would bring to an office

The format does not narrow what you can bring. Anxiety, depression and anger. Trauma and PTSD. Burnout in people whose lives look fine from the outside. The aftermath of domestic violence. Life after military service. Drinking, grief, a marriage that has gone quiet. The full list is on The Work.

If you are in immediate danger or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988, or call 911. The Colorado Crisis Line is 1-844-493-8255, or text TALK to 38255.

Questions Asked before a first call

Before you book an online session.

Do I have to be in Colorado for every session?
Yes. I am licensed in Colorado, and that license covers people who are physically in Colorado at the time of the session. It is a condition of licensure, not a preference. If you will be out of state on a session day, tell me ahead of time and we will work out what is possible.
Is online therapy as good as being in the room?
For most adults and most concerns the research says yes, and that includes trauma work and EMDR. It is not the right fit for an acute crisis, and if that is where you are, I will tell you so plainly and help you find the right level of care.
Does insurance cover online sessions?
Aetna, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield, Carelon, Cigna and Select Health are in network, booked through Headway, which shows your exact cost before you confirm. On another plan you can still be seen, with a receipt each month to send to your insurer. Fees and insurance has the rest.
Are there evening sessions?
Some. They go quickly, so ask about them on the first call.
