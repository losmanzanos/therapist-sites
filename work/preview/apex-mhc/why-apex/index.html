Why Apex

Why a practice is named after a high point.

Where the name came from, why the pages are drawn like a survey, and what any of it has to do with sitting in a room and getting better.

A brain, seen from above
The lines

Why everything here is drawn like a survey.

Look at a brain from above and you are looking at a contour map. That is not a comparison anyone had to invent. A brain folds for the same reason a mountain range does, to pack more surface into the space it has been given. Draw one honestly and you have drawn the other.

So the faint lines behind these pages are contour lines, and they are real ones. They come from public elevation readings for the central Colorado Rockies, the same measurements a topographic map gets printed from. Nothing here is a pattern picked off a shelf.

A map is also where any trip starts. You cannot plan a route until you know where you are standing. Therapy begins the same way, with an honest look at where you are before deciding where you want to go.

Why Apex

Apex means the highest point. You are carrying one.

The brain is the body’s. It’s the summit every other system is built to supply, with blood and oxygen and sleep all running uphill to keep one organ lit.

Colorado is the country’s. More peaks above fourteen thousand feet than any other state, and the highest mean elevation of the fifty. The highest ground in America and the highest ground in a person, both mapped the same way: by tracing the lines where the elevation holds steady.

What apex has never meant is the summit photograph. Ask anyone who has moved here from sea level. You don’t conquer altitude, you acclimatize to it, and the body takes several weeks to manage it.

The elevation that flattened you in August becomes the one you simply live at by October. That is the closer description of this work. Not a peak to reach and post from, but the capacity to hold height in the life you already have.

Not a peak to reach. The capacity to hold height.

Therapy is not a finish line or a perfect final state. It builds the strength to stay steady at altitude: to keep what you have worked for, and to hold your footing through the hard stretches as life keeps changing.
