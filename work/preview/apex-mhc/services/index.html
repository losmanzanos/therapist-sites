The Work

What the work actually looks like.

Not the theory. The first call, the first session, what a normal month is, how long people stay, and how you know it is doing anything.

The first call

Fifteen minutes, free, on the phone. You describe what has been going on in whatever order it comes out. I ask a few questions, and near the end I tell you plainly whether I think I am the right person for it.

Sometimes the answer is no, and that is a useful fifteen minutes rather than a wasted one. I would rather hand you two names of people who fit better than take an intake I have doubts about.

The first session

Sixty minutes, and more structured than the rest will be. I am building a working picture: what is happening now, how long it has been happening, what you have already tried, what the people around you are like, and what you actually want to be different.

You will not be asked to produce your childhood in chronological order. If something from a long time ago is relevant, it will come up on its own, usually because it is still happening in some disguised form on a Tuesday afternoon.

What you leave the first session with

A rough plan, in plain words, and usually one small thing to try. Not a treatment contract full of clinical language you will never reread.

What a normal month looks like

Weekly to begin with, because momentum matters more than people expect and fortnightly sessions often turn into thirty minutes of catching up. Once things are moving, every other week is common, and some people taper further from there.

Between sessions there is usually something small and specific to do. Not worksheets for their own sake. Therapy is one hour out of a hundred and sixty-eight, and the other hundred and sixty-seven are where the actual change has to happen.

How long people stay

Honestly, it varies more than anyone selling a program will admit. A focused problem with a clear shape can move in eight to twelve sessions. Something that has been running for twenty years and shaped how you pick partners takes longer than that, and anyone who promises otherwise is selling something.

What I will not do is keep you in an open-ended arrangement out of habit. If we hit a plateau, I will name it, and we will either change the approach or talk about stopping.

How you know it is working

Rarely as a mood lifting on schedule. More often it shows up sideways, and the people around you notice before you do.

  • The gap between the trigger and your reaction gets slightly wider
  • You recover from a bad day in hours instead of the rest of the week
  • You say the difficult thing once, at normal volume, instead of not at all
  • Something that used to take the whole evening takes twenty minutes
  • You stop rehearsing conversations in the car

When it ends

Deliberately, with a session or two set aside for it, rather than by quietly not rebooking. We look at what changed, what is likely to be the thing that comes back, and what you would do about it if it did.

The door stays open. A fair number of people come back for three or four sessions years later when something specific lands, and that is a normal use of this, not a relapse.

Focused areas Eight of them

Eight of these come up often enough to have their own page.

These are not the only things worked on here. They are the ones where enough people arrive with the same shape of problem that it was worth writing properly about each one.

01

Survivors of domestic violence

The hard part can be deciding to leave, or everything after. Checking the locks, second-guessing yourself, a body that still tenses at certain sounds.

 02

Veterans and service members

A different history, a similar aftermath. I keep it direct, we go at your speed, and you will not have to explain what the job was like first.

 03

Substance use and addiction

Whether it has a name yet or not. No lectures, no scripts, and no requirement that you already know what you want to do about it.

 04

High performers running on empty

You hit the number, cleared the bar, got the title. The relief lasted about a week and you have told almost no one that it did not hold.

 05

Anxiety, depression, and anger

The three that usually arrive together, whichever one you would name first. Anger in particular gets treated as a character flaw when it is usually a signal.

 06

Couples

Two people who can predict the entire argument before it starts, and have it anyway. The pattern is the client, not either of you.

 07

Grief, faith, and life transitions

Loss. Belief that has shifted. A chapter that closed before you were ready for it to.

 08

Trauma and PTSD

A startle out of all proportion, sleep that breaks at the same hour. EMDR if you want it, and you never have to tell the whole story first.

Begin

Not sure which of these is yours?

That is an ordinary place to start, and it is a good use of the consultation call. Describe what has been going on and I will tell you whether I am the right person for it.
