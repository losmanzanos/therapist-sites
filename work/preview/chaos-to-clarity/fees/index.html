Fees and insurance

What a session costs, who I’m in network with, and how to find out what your plan will actually pay before you book.

Rates

$170First session (intake), 60 minutes
$150Ongoing sessions, 53 minutes or more

Same rate in person or by telehealth. Payment is due at the time of session: cash, credit or debit card, HSA or FSA card, or electronic payment.

If you’re using insurance, you pay your copay or coinsurance instead, and I bill your plan for the rest.

Plans change what they cover more often than you’d think, and “in network” doesn’t always mean “free.” Before your first session, call the member services number on the back of your card and ask:

  1. Is Brooke Bloker, LPC, in network for outpatient mental health?

    Give them my NPI if they ask. I’ll send it to you when we book.

  2. What’s my copay or coinsurance for an outpatient therapy visit?

    Ask about CPT codes 90791 (intake) and 90837 (53-minute session).

  3. Do I have a deductible, and how much is left on it?

    Until it’s met, you may pay the full contracted rate, which is often less than my cash rate.

  4. Is telehealth covered the same as in person?

    Most plans say yes now. A few still differ.

  5. Do I need a referral or prior authorization?

    Rare for therapy, but worth asking.

Write down the date, the rep’s name, and a reference number. If a claim is ever denied, that call is your best evidence.

Out of network

If your plan isn’t on the list, you pay my rate and I give you a superbill.

A superbill is a detailed receipt with the codes your insurer needs. You submit it to your plan (usually through their website or app) and they reimburse you directly for whatever your out-of-network benefit covers. For many PPO plans that’s 50 to 80 percent of the session fee after your out-of-network deductible is met.

To find out what yours would pay, call the same number and ask about “out-of-network outpatient mental health benefits” for the two codes above. HMO and Medicaid plans usually don’t have out-of-network benefits at all.

I can send superbills monthly, so you’re not submitting after every session.

Crime Victims Compensation

If you’ve been the victim of a crime in Larimer County, including domestic violence, sexual assault, or childhood abuse, the county’s Crime Victims Compensation program may pay for your counseling. I’m an approved provider, so there’s no superbill and nothing out of pocket once you’re approved.

The program is run through the District Attorney’s office. Applying usually means a police report or other documentation of the crime, and there are time limits, though they’re more flexible for abuse that happened in childhood. The victim advocate at the DA’s office can walk you through it, and so can I.

If this applies to you, say so on the consult call and we’ll start there.

Policies

  • Cancellations need at least 24 hours’ notice. Less than that, or a missed session, is a $100 fee. Insurance doesn’t cover that fee, so it’s billed to you directly. If you’re sick or the weather turns, call me. I’d rather move you to video than charge you.
  • Running late? If you’re more than 10 minutes late without a text or call, the session is canceled and counts as a no-show. Up to 10 minutes late, we use the time that’s left.
  • Payment is due at the time of session. I keep a card on file for copays and fees.
  • Sliding scale. I don’t have a formal one, but if cost is the thing standing between you and getting started, bring it up on the consult call.

Your right to a Good Faith Estimate

Under the federal No Surprises Act, if you aren’t using insurance you have the right to a written estimate of what your care will cost before you start, and at any point after. Mine is simple: $170 for the first session and $150 for each session after that, times however many sessions we expect. I’ll give you one when we schedule, and you can ask for an updated one anytime.

If you’re ever billed $400 or more above the estimate, you can dispute it. Details are at cms.gov/nosurprises.

Bring them to the free consult. Fifteen minutes on the phone is usually enough to sort out what you’d actually pay.
