Individual therapy for adults

Weekly sessions for adults dealing with trauma, anxiety, depression, grief, codependency, or relationship patterns that keep repeating. In person in Fort Collins, or by telehealth in Colorado, Iowa, and Arizona.

Book a free 15-minute consult

What people bring in

Trauma is usually somewhere in the picture, but it doesn’t have to be the headline. Plenty of people start with something that just won’t ease up.

  • Anxiety that hums in the background, even on good days.

  • Grief that didn’t follow the stages anyone promised.

  • Saying yes when you mean no, then resenting it for a week.

  • Feelings that go from zero to a hundred with nothing in between.

  • The same relationship, over and over, with different people.

How I approach it

Your story and your particular mix of struggles are yours, so I don’t run everyone through the same method.

I combine traditional talk therapy (psychodynamic, cognitive-behavioral, and narrative work) with mindfulness, body-based approaches, and EMDR when it fits. Early sessions are about getting the lay of the land: what’s happening now, what’s helped before, what hasn’t. Then we pick a direction together, and I’ll be straight with you about what I think will help.

Some weeks we’ll practice a specific skill. Some weeks you’ll need to talk about what happened on Tuesday. Both count.

Women-specific care

I spent years in women’s inpatient treatment, and I still offer care built around women’s experiences: reproductive health, trauma, trauma-focused substance use treatment, and reunification support for mothers working to rebuild things with their kids.

What a session looks like

We start with where you are today, not where we left off. Sometimes that’s the whole hour, and that’s fine. More often it’s ten minutes, and then we pick up a thread: a pattern we’ve been tracking, a skill we’re building, or a memory we agreed to work on.

I keep an eye on your body as much as your words. If your shoulders climb or your voice flattens, I’ll probably point it out, and we may slow down right there. Noticing in real time is a big part of how this works.

The last few minutes are for landing. You shouldn’t leave my office raw. If something big came up, we close it enough that you can drive home and make dinner.

How we know it’s working

  • The gap between a trigger and your reaction gets wider. You catch yourself sooner.
  • You can talk about the thing without your body going back there.
  • Fewer decisions get made from panic.
  • You start saying no earlier, and feeling less guilty about it later.
  • You spend less time in session on crisis and more on what you want.

We check in on this every so often, out loud. If it isn’t working, we change the plan.
