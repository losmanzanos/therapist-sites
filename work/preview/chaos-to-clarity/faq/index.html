Questions people ask

The short answers to what most people want to know before booking. If yours isn’t here, ask on the free consult.

Getting started

What if I’m not sure it’s “trauma”?

You don’t need a label to book a consult. If something keeps happening that you can’t explain or stop, that’s reason enough to talk.

What happens on the free consult?

Fifteen minutes on the phone. You tell me a little about what’s going on, I tell you how I’d approach it, and we both get a feel for whether it’s a fit. If it isn’t, I’ll help you find someone who is.

Who do you work with?

Adults. Most of my clients are working through complex trauma, dissociation, relational abuse, codependency, or personality-related patterns like BPD. I’m LGBTQIA+, trans and nonbinary, polyamory and CNM, and BIPOC affirming.

Do I need a diagnosis first?

No. Plenty of people come in without one. If a diagnosis would be useful, for insurance or for making sense of things, we can talk about it together.

Sessions

How long are sessions, and how often?

Your first session is a 60-minute intake. After that, sessions run 53 minutes or more, usually weekly to start. Some people move to every other week once things are steadier.

Do you offer telehealth?

Yes, over secure video anywhere in Colorado, Iowa, or Arizona. In-person sessions are at my office on South Timberline Road in Fort Collins.

Do I have to talk about what happened in detail?

No. A lot of trauma work, EMDR included, doesn’t need a blow-by-blow account. We go at the speed your system can handle, and you decide what gets said out loud.

How long does therapy take?

It depends on what you’re carrying and how much stabilization you need first. Some people come for a few months, others for longer. We check in on how it’s going regularly, and you’re never locked in.

Is EMDR right for everyone?

Not right away, and not always. If you dissociate, or if there isn’t much stability in your life yet, we build that first and adapt EMDR when we get there. More on the EMDR page.

Cost and insurance

How much does it cost?

The first session (intake) is $170. Ongoing sessions are $150.

Which insurance do you take?

Anthem BCBS Colorado, Aetna, Cigna, Carelon, Quest Behavioral Health, UHC/UMR, Wellmark BCBS, Horizon BCBS of New Jersey, BCBS of Massachusetts, and Larimer County Crime Victims Compensation. The fees page has the questions to ask your plan before your first session.

What if my insurance isn’t on the list?

I can give you a superbill to submit for out-of-network reimbursement. HSA and FSA cards work too.

What’s your cancellation policy?

Cancel at least 24 hours ahead to avoid a $100 late-cancellation fee. More than 10 minutes late without a heads-up counts as a no-show.

What’s a Good Faith Estimate?

If you’re not using insurance, federal law gives you the right to an estimate of what your care will cost before you start. Ask me for one anytime. Details are at cms.gov/nosurprises.

Mac

Is Mac there every session?

Most days, yes. Mac is a golden retriever and a working therapy dog, usually on the rug and usually more interested in your shoes than your history.

What if I’m allergic, or not a dog person?

Just mention it when you book. Mac is happy to wait next door.

Privacy and safety

Is what I say confidential?

Yes, with a few limits set by law, like a serious risk of harm to you or someone else, or suspected abuse of a child or vulnerable adult. I go over all of it at your first session so there are no surprises.

Can I share details through the contact form or email?

Please don’t. Email and web forms aren’t secure channels. Send a sentence or two to get started, and we’ll talk specifics on the phone or in session.

What if I’m in crisis?

This practice isn’t a crisis service. If you’re in crisis, call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, or call 911.
