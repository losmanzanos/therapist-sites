Curio · working marks

Six ways to say "worth a second look."

Every option here is a wordmark plus one small mark that can live on its own as a favicon, a social avatar, or a bullet. Nothing mystical, nothing medical. Shown on light, dark and honey so you can see how each one travels.

01

The spark

Her sketch, minus the eye. A four-point glint, the moment something hidden catches light. Reads as "aha," not as an all-seeing anything.

Why it works: it's the one piece of her concept that everyone understood. Simple enough to stamp on a business card at 6mm.

02

The keyhole C

The C of the wordmark is the keyhole. Curio cabinets lock; therapy is where you get the key. Ties straight back to the name without needing a cabinet on screen.

Why it works: a monogram clients remember, and the keyhole alone is a strong favicon.

03

The loupe

A jeweler's glass with the spark inside it. Curiosity as an action: pick the thing up, look closer. Keeps her spark and her "second look" idea without an eye.

Watch out: magnifying glasses also mean "search." The spark inside is what keeps it from reading as a search bar.

04

Two drawers

The quietest option. A small chest of two drawers with brass pulls. It's the cabinet reduced to a glyph: things kept, things you can open. Clear at any size.

Why it works: no symbolism to misread. Looks like a good furniture or stationery brand, which is the right company for a practice that wants to feel established.

05

Letter tiles

The shop sign from the first Curio preview, carried forward as the logo. Five tiles, one in honey. The site changes; the thing she already liked stays.

Why it works: continuity with what she's seen, and a single tile makes a perfect square avatar.

06

Her eye and spark (for comparison)

Sarah's sketch, drawn clean. Shown here so the other five can be judged against it on the same backgrounds.

The honest problem: an eye with a star in it reads as tarot, optometry, or surveillance before it reads as therapy, and the all-seeing-eye association is exactly the "spiritual" read she said she wants to avoid. The spark is doing all the real work in it.

How these sit next to the two homepage directions

Options 1, 3 and 4 were drawn for the parchment-and-ink direction. Options 2 and 5 were drawn for the green-and-brass direction. All six work in either palette; the colors above are just the first fit. Whichever mark she picks, the wordmark stays a plain, well-set "Curio" so the name does the talking.
