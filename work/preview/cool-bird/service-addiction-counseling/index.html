You don’t have to call it addiction to talk to me about it. A lot of people start with a smaller question, like whether the drinking has crept up, or why the plan to cut back keeps falling apart by Thursday.

I’m licensed in Colorado as both a Licensed Addiction Counselor (LAC) and a Licensed Professional Counselor (LPC). In plain terms, one person can work on the substance use and on whatever sits underneath it: grief, depression, anxiety, trauma, anger, codependency. Those almost never arrive one at a time, and I don’t treat them one at a time.

Outside this practice I serve as Clinical Director at Denver Recovery Solutions, so level-of-care questions are part of my everyday work. If weekly counseling isn’t enough for where you are, I’ll tell you plainly and point you toward what would be.

You don’t have to want to quit first

Abstinence is one good goal among several. If harm reduction is where you are, that’s where we start. Some people come in certain they want to stop entirely. Others want to use less, or use more safely, or understand why they use the way they do before deciding anything at all.

I’m not going to hand you a verdict on your use before we’ve even talked about it. More on harm reduction and abstinence →

Who I see for this

  • Adults and adolescents concerned about their alcohol or drug use
  • People in early recovery, or starting again after a relapse
  • People who aren’t sure recovery is the right word yet
  • Anyone whose use is tangled up with grief, a loss to overdose, depression, or anxiety
  • People who have been asked for a formal evaluation, which starts with an assessment

What the sessions are like

Sessions run 45 to 60 minutes over Zoom for Healthcare. Most people start weekly and taper as things steady out. The early ones cover what you use, how much, when, and what it does for you. That last part matters, because substances usually work at first, and pretending otherwise makes the conversation useless.

From there you set the goals. You should leave each session with something concrete to try before the next one.

Need a formal evaluation? If a court, probation officer, employer, or licensing board asked for one, start with a substance use and mental health assessment: $150, 60–90 minutes, with written findings. Counseling can follow if it fits.

Some people pair counseling with AA, NA, SMART Recovery, or Al-Anon. Some don’t. The resources page has meeting finders for each, plus 988 and the SAMHSA helpline for the nights that can’t wait for a session.

Common questions

About addiction counseling

What is the difference between an LAC and an LPC?

An LPC is Colorado’s license for professional counselors who treat mental health concerns. An LAC is the state’s license specifically for addiction counseling. I hold both. Any Colorado license can be checked through the DORA License Lookup.

Will anyone else find out I’m in counseling?

Information is released only where you authorize it in writing, and the Notice of Privacy Practices spells out the exceptions the law requires. Some people choose private pay so that no diagnosis or treatment information goes on record with an insurance company.

Do you work with teenagers who are using?

Yes. I work with adolescents and adults on substance use. Adolescent clients are seen with the consents Colorado law requires.

What if I relapse while we’re working together?

Then that’s what we talk about next. A relapse tells us something about what the plan was missing, and it’s one of the moments people most often reach out in the first place.

How much does addiction counseling cost?

Sessions are $100 for 45–60 minutes. Network participation changes from time to time, so ask me where it stands before you book. A Good Faith Estimate is available on request.
