Grief Counseling

$100

45–60 minute session

Book a consultation

Grief doesn’t keep office hours. It catches you in the cereal aisle, or at 3 a.m. with the phone in your hand.

Some people want to talk about the person they lost from the first minute. Others need a few sessions on anything else before they can get near it. Both are fine. We go at the pace you can carry, and nobody is grading how well you’re grieving.

When the loss was an overdose or a suicide

These deaths bring questions that ordinary grief usually doesn’t. Whether you missed something. Whether you’re allowed to be angry at them. What to say when a coworker asks how they died. Whether the relief you feel on some days makes you a bad person.

People often carry those alone, because the subject makes a room go quiet. I’m also a Licensed Addiction Counselor, and much of my week is spent around substance use and recovery. Talking about their use, or the years of worry before the overdose, won’t make me flinch.

Grief and substance use tend to show up together. For some people the drinking starts after the funeral. For others, a loss knocks loose a recovery that had been holding for years. Addiction, grief, and mental health rarely show up separately, so I don’t treat them separately. If that’s part of your picture, addiction counseling happens in the same sessions.

Who this is for

  • Adults grieving a death, recent or long past
  • Adolescents after the death of someone close to them
  • Anyone who lost someone to overdose or suicide
  • People whose grief has gotten mixed up with drinking, drug use, depression, or anxiety

If grief has turned into thoughts of not wanting to be here, call or text 988 any hour of the day. A safety plan written on a calmer day can help on the harder ones.

For younger children, Judi’s House offers grief support for Colorado kids and families, especially after a sudden loss. It’s listed on the resources page with the other places I point people.

Common questions

About grief counseling

Is it too soon, or too late, to start grief counseling?

Neither. People start in the first weeks after a death, and people start years later when a loss finally catches up with them. If it’s on your mind enough that you’re reading this, it’s a reasonable time to call.

How long does grief counseling last?

Most people start weekly and taper as things steady out. Some come for a few months around the loss itself; others stay longer. You set the goals, and we check together whether it’s still helping.

Do I have to talk about how they died?

No. You decide what we cover and when. It’s fine to spend the first sessions on everything around the loss before getting anywhere near the loss itself.

What does grief counseling cost?

Sessions are $100 for 45–60 minutes over Zoom for Healthcare. Network participation changes from time to time, so ask me where it stands.
