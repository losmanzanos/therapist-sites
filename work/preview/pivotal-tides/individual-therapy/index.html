Where People Start

Most people come in with one thing that won’t let go

Sometimes it’s a loss. Sometimes it’s months of nothing being technically wrong while you still don’t feel like yourself. Very few people arrive with the whole picture, and you don’t need it to make the call.

The three areas I focus on most are women’s issues, infertility, and grief. I also see adults for anxiety and depression, stress and burnout, anger, self-esteem, questions of identity and purpose, recovery from narcissistic abuse, and life during and after cancer.

If yours isn’t on that list, call anyway.

What People Bring

A closer look at the work

Being a woman, at whatever stage you’re in. I’m a Certified Women’s Mental Health & Reproductive Mental Health Specialist, and women’s issues sit at the center of my practice. Some of that is fertility and reproductive health. A lot of it is the particular weight women carry for everyone else, and what happens when there’s no room left for you.

If trying to get pregnant is the thing taking up the most space, there’s a page on infertility counseling and surrogacy support.

Grief, including the kind nobody sees. A death, a divorce, a diagnosis, a friendship that faded out. More on grief counseling.

After a narcissistic relationship. I’m certified in narcissistic abuse treatment. After that kind of relationship, it can take a while to trust your own memory of what happened. We can start right there, with what you remember and what it cost you. More on narcissistic abuse recovery.

Anger. I’m certified in anger management too. Anger often sits on top of something harder to feel, so we work with both.

Anxiety, depression, burnout. We look at what keeps them going in your actual week, the thoughts and habits and pressures, and at what’s underneath.

Cancer. Cancer psychotherapy is part of the practice, for life during treatment and for the strange stretch after it.

Transitions and identity. A move, a career you had to give up, a child leaving home, a marriage ending. When the life you built changes shape, it’s normal to lose track of who you are in it for a while.

In Session

Your story first, tools second

We start with how you got here, what matters most to you, and what tends to get in your way. The practical tools come after that, built around your actual life.

My approach is depth-oriented and insight-focused. I draw on Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for the structural side, meaning the thoughts and beliefs that quietly run the day. Client-Centered Therapy covers the rest, the grief or longing or doubt that doesn’t have a tidy answer and mostly needs to be heard.

Some people come weekly. Others settle into every other week, or monthly. We figure that out together and adjust it as you go.

More on how I work. A support group can also run alongside individual sessions.

Common Questions

Before your first session

How much is individual therapy?

+

$175 for a 50-minute session, paid at the time of service by card or check. Aetna, Anthem Blue Cross, Carelon and Cigna/Evernorth are accepted, along with out-of-network benefits. If cost is what’s stopping you, call and ask about payment arrangements.

Can I do individual therapy online?

+

Yes. Sessions happen at the Temecula office on Ridge Park Drive or over secure, HIPAA-compliant video, and you can tell me on the consultation call which one suits you.

How often would we meet?

+

It depends on what you’re working on. Some people come weekly, others every other week or once a month. We’ll decide together during the consultation and change it as things shift.

Do I need to know what I want to work on before I call?

+

No. A lot of people start with something like “I just don’t feel like myself.” That’s enough to begin with. The free 15-minute consultation is there to sort out the rest.
