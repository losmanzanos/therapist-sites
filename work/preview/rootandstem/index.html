01 Why we started this

The trainings that changed how we practice were never the big ones.

Six hours in a hotel conference room. A slide deck read out loud. A certificate that turns up in your inbox eleven days later. You leave with the hours you needed and nothing you’ll use on Monday.

The ones that stuck were small. Fifteen people, maybe twenty. Someone teaching who still saw clients on Thursdays, who would stop halfway through a slide and say here’s where I got this wrong for about six years.

That kind of room is hard to schedule and impossible to scale, which is probably why so few people bother running them.

We kept waiting for someone else to build it. Full Bloom already had most of the pieces: a practice full of clinicians who consult with each other every week, and a habit of taking that seriously enough to protect the time. The Root and Stem is that habit, opened up to the people who don’t work here.

So: small rooms. Teachers who are still in the work. Enough time that the good conversation, the one that usually starts in the parking lot afterward, happens inside the room instead.

02

Four ways in

Some of it is about getting better at the work. Some of it is about being able to keep doing the work at all.

  1. i

    Trainings & Workshops

    Half and full days on the things that come up in consultation over and over: couples work where one person has quietly checked out, the Enneagram used clinically rather than as a parlor game, weight-neutral care, trauma work that has stopped moving. Bring a case. There is time to talk about it.

    In person & onlineCE where offered

  2. ii

    Retreats

    Three days somewhere with poor cell service. Mornings hold teaching, afternoons hold nothing on purpose, and meals are together with attendance optional. You will not be asked to introduce yourself with a fun fact.

  3. iii

    Consultation Groups

    Eight clinicians, monthly, held together for a full year. The continuity is the point. Somewhere around month four, people stop presenting their competence and start bringing the case that’s keeping them up at night. Any license, any setting.

    MonthlyYear-long cohort

  4. iv

    Supervision & Mentorship

    Individual and dyadic hours toward LPC and LMFT licensure with an AAMFT Approved Supervisor. Practice mentorship too: rates, notes, referrals, and the business questions graduate school skipped.

03

You don’t need another training.

You need space to reconnect.

A retreat for the person behind the therapist. Therapists are excellent at prescribing rest and dismal at taking it. These are built on the assumption that you already know what you need and haven’t had three consecutive days to go and find it.

  1. i

    Mornings hold the teaching. One topic, taken slowly, with room to argue with it.

  2. ii

    Afternoons are yours. Walk, sleep, sit by a window, read something unrelated to the field.

  3. iii

    Evenings are a long table and other clinicians who understand the shorthand.

The first one is November 4–7, 2027, in Estes Park, close enough to Denver to drive, far enough that the phone stops working properly. Details and early access go to the waitlist before they go anywhere else.

04

How we run things

Small on purpose

Cohorts are capped so everyone gets to talk and nobody can hide at the back. Keeping rooms this size costs us money. It’s the whole product.

Taught from the chair

Everyone teaching here carries a caseload. The examples are current, the theory has been tested on a Tuesday afternoon, and nobody is presenting from memory.

Rest counts

We schedule time to do nothing and don’t apologize for it.

07

Before you sign up

Do these count for continuing education credit?

CE approval is in progress. Once it’s confirmed, the hours and the approving body will be listed on each training that carries credit. On the ones that don’t, we’ll say so. Retreats are built around rest rather than contact hours, so most of those won’t carry CE. We’d rather be plain about it than stretch a definition to make a weekend sound deductible.

Who are the trainings for?

Licensed therapists, pre-licensed clinicians accruing hours, and graduate students in a clinical program. A few offerings are aimed at a narrower group (supervisors, practice owners, clinicians already trained in a particular modality), and those say so at the top. If you’re unsure, email and describe your setting.

What does a retreat weekend look like?

You arrive Thursday afternoon and settle in. Friday and Saturday mornings hold three hours of teaching. Afternoons are unscheduled. Meals are together and optional. You leave Sunday morning.

Do I have to be connected to Full Bloom?

No. Most people who come through are from other practices, agencies, hospital systems or solo work. Full Bloom clinicians are in the room too, which tends to make the case discussion better rather than more insular.

Is there financial assistance?

Two seats in every cohort are held at a reduced rate for clinicians in their first few years, or working in community mental health, or in a stretch of year where the full fee isn’t happening. There’s no application and no essay. You email and ask, and we say yes until the seats are gone.

Can you bring a training to our practice?

Yes. We run private trainings for group practices, agencies and clinical teams, at your site or ours. Tell us the size of your team, what you keep running into, and roughly what you have to work with.

What’s the cancellation policy?

Full refund up to 30 days before. Half up to 14 days. After that it becomes credit toward a future offering. Illness, a family emergency, a client crisis you can’t hand off: those get handled case by case, and we have never been rigid about it.
