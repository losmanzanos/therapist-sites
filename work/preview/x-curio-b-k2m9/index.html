What we help with

Most people don't walk in with a diagnosis.

They walk in with a question they've been turning over for a while. Curio comes from the Latin curiosus: careful, inquiring. A curio is a small thing worth picking up and looking at again. That's the practice. These are the six questions we hear most, and the four ways we work on them.

01

Anxiety and stress

For the 3 a.m. list and the shoulders up by your ears.
02

How we work

EMDR

A way to work through a memory without retelling every detail of it. Sarah has taught it to other therapists since 2018.

 

EMDR intensives

Consecutive days of EMDR with Sarah, alongside your regular therapist, for people who want to move faster.

 

Sand tray

You build a scene with miniatures. It often says what words can't reach yet.

 

Mindfulness skills group

An eight-week online group with Lora. Skills you keep, in a room of people working on the same thing.

Why Curio

Why people choose a small practice.

  1. You see the same person every week, and she answers to Sarah.

    Curio isn't a directory or a platform. Sarah Freeze owns it, supervises every case, and trains other EMDR therapists for a living. Lora works under that supervision.

  2. We take your insurance.

    Kaiser, UnitedHealthcare, Oxford, Optum, Oscar, Surest, UMR, UHC Student Resources, and Colorado Medicaid. For anything else, a superbill. Fees and insurance.

  3. Curious, not clinical.

    We're interested in where a pattern started, not in giving it a label and a worksheet. You can ask questions and tell us when something isn't working. That's how the practice runs.

  4. Depth, at your pace.

    Psychodynamic, attachment-based, and trauma-trained. Some people come weekly for a year. Some do a three-day intensive. Both are real therapy.
