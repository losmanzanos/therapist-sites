Curiosity no. 04 · Trauma and PTSD

Was it bad enough to count as trauma?

Trauma and PTSD therapy in Denver

People ask this a lot, usually right after describing something that would flatten anyone. If you’re still carrying it, it counts.

What it can look like

  • A smell or a tone of voice puts you right back there.
  • You avoid a street, a person, a kind of conversation.
  • You startle easily, sleep badly, or feel numb where you used to feel things.
  • You grew up fast because someone had to be the adult.

How we work with trauma

Trauma is the center of this practice. Sarah Freeze has spent her career with adults who were abused or neglected as children, and she supervises every clinician here.

Treatment starts slowly on purpose. Before anyone asks you to go back into a memory, you and your therapist build the footing for it: what helps you settle, who is on your side, how to stop when you need to stop.

From there the approach depends on you. Curio uses EMDR, psychodynamic therapy, relational work, and sand tray. You won’t be pushed to tell the whole story before you’re ready, and with some methods you never have to say all of it out loud.

Questions people ask

Does it have to be one big event?

No. A lot of trauma is quieter than that. Years of being ignored, criticized, or scared at home can do as much as a single terrible day.

Will I have to talk about everything?

No. You set the pace. Some approaches, EMDR among them, don’t require you to describe a memory in detail.

Do you work with survivors of sexual abuse?

Yes. Sarah has specific training in sexual abuse, sexual assault, and incest, and that experience shapes how the whole practice works.
