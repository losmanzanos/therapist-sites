Curiosity no. 09 · Sand tray therapy

What do you do with a tray of sand?

Sand tray therapy in Denver

You build a scene. There’s a tray of sand and shelves of miniature figures: people, animals, houses, fences, monsters, a tiny chair. You pick what catches you and put it where it wants to go.

Ask about sand tray
Sand tray therapy

Why it works

Some things are hard to say because you don’t have the words yet, or because the words have been said so many times they’ve gone flat. A scene in the sand skips past that. You see the whole situation at once, from above, with your hands still in it.

Then you and your therapist look at it together. Who is close to whom. What’s buried. What is facing the wrong way. People are often surprised by what they made.

Who it’s for

Sand tray is for adults as much as children. It helps when talking has stalled, when a memory is too much to approach head on, or when you tend to think your way around feelings. It can sit alongside EMDR and psychodynamic work.

It’s one of Sarah Freeze’s specialties, and the practice’s collection of miniatures lives at the Harlan Street office. Because it’s hands-on, it happens in person.

Questions people ask

Do I need to be creative?

No. There’s no good or bad tray.

Is this play therapy?

It’s related. Adults use it because it gets to things that talking can’t.
