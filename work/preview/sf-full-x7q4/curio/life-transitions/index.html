Curiosity no. 03 · Life transitions

Who am I after the divorce?

Therapy for life transitions in Denver

A divorce. A career you’re leaving, or one that left you. Menopause. A quiet house after the last kid moves out. The life you built yourself around has changed shape.

Book a 15-minute call with Lora

Look closer. Things change up close.

Léon Cogniet, The Artist in His Room at the Villa Medici, Rome, 1817. Cleveland Museum of Art.

How it shows up

  • You keep saying you’re fine and people keep believing you.
  • You don’t recognize your own days anymore.
  • There’s grief and there’s relief, and you feel guilty about the relief.
  • You’re not sure what you want, because for years that wasn’t the question.

Where we start

Lora works with women in the middle of exactly these changes. The first sessions are usually about getting the whole story out, including the parts that don’t fit the version you tell friends.

Then the questions get more interesting. What did the old role give you? What did it cost? What do you want the next ten years to be for?

She uses psychodynamic and attachment-based therapy, which means paying attention to patterns that started long before this change did.

When to come in

You don’t have to be falling apart. Many people start while the change is still on its way, because a divorce or a retirement goes better with somewhere to think out loud.

Questions people ask

Do you see couples?

Curio offers individual therapy. If couples work would help, Lora can talk with you about where to look.

Is menopause really something to bring to therapy?

Yes. It changes sleep, mood, and how you feel in your own body, often right when other things are shifting too. It belongs in the room.
