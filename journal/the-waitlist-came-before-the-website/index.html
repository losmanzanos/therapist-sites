The waitlist came before the website

By Chad Moravec  ·  November 9, 2026

The Root and Stem is a branch of Full Bloom Counseling, the Denver group practice on channel 02. It runs small trainings, year-long consultation groups, supervision hours toward licensure, and retreats, all for therapists. By August it had a name and a logo. It did not have dates, a list of who would teach, or CE approval.

That’s the awkward stretch for anything new. You want people to know it’s coming, and you don’t have enough to fill a real website yet. A site with “coming soon” stamped on every section looks unfinished. Waiting until everything is settled means launching to an empty room.

So I built both, and only showed one.

August: a greenhouse with the light on

The first version was one screen. A dark page, an old engraved greenhouse, and a single line: “Someone left the light on.” A few seconds after the page loads, the glass warms up. Click “Come in” and the greenhouse glows brighter while a two-line form takes the button’s place.

No menu, no list of offerings, nothing to compare against anything. That was the point. A page with one thing on it has one thing to click, and a curious therapist doesn’t have much to do there except leave a first name and an email.

The parts you can’t see took longer than the greenhouse did. The form posts straight to Kit, the email tool, so it works even with JavaScript off. And the full site already existed. It sat at a hidden address with a noindex tag on it, so Google couldn’t find it early and decide that was the real home page before it was ready.

October 28: same list, bigger house

When the details firmed up, the full site moved to the front door. “Before the bloom, the root.” One long page in six parts: why it exists, the offerings, retreats, a calendar, who teaches, and an FAQ. The first retreat got a real date, November 4 to 7, 2027, in Estes Park, plus an event listing in the page’s structured data so search results can show it with the date attached.

Here’s the part I’d steal if I were you. The signup form on the new site posts to the same Kit list the greenhouse did. Nobody who joined in August had to join again, and nobody fell through a crack during the switch. The greenhouse moved to its own address and the old hidden link now redirects home. Kit sends the welcome email the moment someone signs up.

Related, not a copy

Because it grew out of Full Bloom, the new site had to feel related without looking like a page Full Bloom forgot to finish. Full Bloom is cream and petal pink. The Root and Stem went dark: deep forest and clay, film grain, its own typefaces, an editorial grid with hanging numbers. The logo is a stem holding a bud that hasn’t opened yet, because the whole idea is “before the bloom.”

The family resemblance mostly lives where people don’t look. Under the logo it says “A branch of Full Bloom Counseling.” In the structured data, Full Bloom is listed as the parent organization, which tells Google the two sites belong together instead of competing for the same searches.

Launch the list first. The website can catch up.

If you’re starting something

A training, a group, a consultation offering, a second location, a new name. The pattern works for any of them. Put up one good-looking page with one job and a real email list behind it. Build the full site while the list grows, and keep it out of search until it’s ready. Then launch onto the same list, so the people who raised their hand first hear about it first.

If you’re reading this for the trainings and not the web design, fair enough. The waitlist is open.
