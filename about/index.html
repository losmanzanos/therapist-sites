About

One person, in Denver, who answers the phone.

Therapist Web Design is Chad Moravec. No account manager, no team page with stock photos, no "we." If you call the number in the header, this is who picks up.

Who you'd be working with

I actually like doing this the hard way.

Most therapists didn't go into private practice to fight a website builder at ten o'clock on a Sunday night. You'd rather spend that time on clients. Or asleep. That's the gap I work in.

I'm Chad, a Denver-based developer whose wife runs her own group practice. I built her site. I know this world from the inside: the directories, the niche specialties, the way clients actually find you, the language that lands and the language that doesn't.

Every site I build is hand-coded from scratch. No themes. No page builders. No two practices ever get the same template, because no two practices are the same. What you get is a fast little website that's yours: your domain, your hosting, your code, your name on everything.

You pay once. No fee that creeps up every January, and no company that can fold and take your site down with it.

Before this I was a classroom teacher in Colorado, which turns out to be the right training for explaining canonical tags to someone who did not ask to learn about canonical tags. I write the way I'd want it explained to me.

I work from Denver. Most of my clients are in Colorado, a few are in Florida and California, and all of it happens over Zoom and email, with a phone call whenever you'd rather talk.

Therapists are the specialty, not the limit. The same hand-built approach has gone into a property-management firm and a timber-framing shop, and the web applications, client portals and hardware experiments live under my other name, Studio Null. Think of it as the whole workshop; this site is the bench where the therapist work gets done.

How it's built, for the people who want to know

Nothing here is rented.

You don't need to understand any of this to hire me. It's here because some people like to see the receipts. Every piece ends up in your name.

Static HTML / CSS / JS

There's no database and no plugin waiting on an update. Less code means less to break and less for anyone to break into, and the site stays fast for years without me touching it.

Nothing to update
GitHub

Every version of your site lives in a repository in your name. If you ever hire someone else, you hand them access and they can start that afternoon.

Your code · your repo
Cloudflare

Your site is served from Cloudflare's network, close to whoever is visiting, with their security sitting in front of it. A practice site fits comfortably in the free tier.

Hosting: $0 a month
TinaCMS · optional

An optional editor for changing your own words between sessions, like a bio, your hours or a new post. No code, and no monthly subscription.

Edit it yourself, or just call me
Contact forms

A form that matches your site and sends inquiries to your inbox, with spam filtering. If people might type clinical details into it, it runs through a service that signs a BAA instead, often your EHR's own. Why that matters.

HIPAA, thought through
SEO & structured data

Schema, a sitemap, an llms.txt file and local search setup go in with the first line of code, so Google and AI assistants can tell what you do and where you do it.

Findable from day one
